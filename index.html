Interactive archival maps · 1804–1807

Bedford: Passages, 1804–1807

Trace the second and third transatlantic voyages of the Bedford, a British ship involved in trafficking enslaved African people, through historical maps and records held by The National Archives.

This resource is associated with PASSAGE (Partnership for Atlantic Slavery Scholarship, Archiving and Global Exchange), a collaborative research programme funded by Lloyd’s Register Foundation. PASSAGE combines research into the histories of enslavement with an international programme supporting researchers and archivists in West Africa and the Caribbean.

About this collection

Three interactive map narratives present the Bedford’s second passage (1804–1805), third passage (1806–1807), and stops in Jamaica. Routes are shown on a 1719 world map, with additional locations on a historical map of Jamaica.

Archival sources: C 114/158; T 71/119, T 71/65 and T 71/164; T 70/1583 and T 70/1584/74. Map references: WO 78/419 and CO 700/Jamaica 36.

Content warning

This resource examines histories of transatlantic enslavement and includes quotations from historical records containing dehumanising and distressing descriptions of enslaved people.
